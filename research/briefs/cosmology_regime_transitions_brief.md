## 1. Cosmological Phase Transitions: Metastability Made Cosmic

### 1.1 Electroweak transition — a *crossover*, not a first-order transition (in the Standard Model)
- Nonperturbative lattice studies (3D dimensionally-reduced effective theory) established that the SM electroweak transition is first-order only for light Higgs masses and terminates at a critical endpoint around m_H ≈ 72–80 GeV; at the physical Higgs mass (125 GeV) it is a **smooth crossover**.
  - Kajantie, Laine, Rummukainen, Shaposhnikov, "The electroweak phase transition: a nonperturbative analysis," *Nucl. Phys. B* 466 (1996) 189 [hep-lat/9510020].
  - Kajantie et al., "Is there a hot electroweak phase transition at m_H ≳ m_W?", *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 77 (1996) 2887.
  - Csikor, Fodor, Heitger, "Endpoint of the hot electroweak phase transition," *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 82 (1999) 21 — the endpoint is a genuine **critical point (second-order)**, the electroweak analog of the liquid–gas critical point.
  - D'Onofrio & Rummukainen, "Standard model cross-over on the lattice," *Phys. Rev. D* 93 (2016) 025003.
- **Structural reading**: the SM sits past the critical endpoint of a fold/cusp structure in (m_H, T) parameter space — the universe *crossed the cusp's shoulder*, not the bistable region. First-order EW transitions require beyond-SM physics (singlet extensions, 2HDM), which restore the cusp.

### 1.2 QCD transition — crossover at μ_B = 0, with a conjectured critical endpoint
- Aoki, Endrődi, Fodor, Katz, Szabó, "The order of the quantum chromodynamics transition predicted by the standard model of particle physics," *Nature* 443 (2006) 675: at physical quark masses and zero chemical potential, the QCD transition is an **analytic crossover** (T_c ≈ 155–160 MeV, Borsanyi et al. 2020).
- A **QCD critical endpoint** at finite baryon density is conjectured (Stephanov, Rajagopal, Shuryak, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 81 (1998) 4816, on tricritical-point signatures) — actively hunted in heavy-ion collisions (STAR BES). Status: **unconfirmed, contested**.
- **Structural reading**: the QCD phase diagram is hypothesized to contain a cusp catastrophe in (T, μ_B) — the crossover line is the "handle" of the cusp, the critical endpoint its cusp point. Heavy-ion programs are literally searching for **critical opalescence** (enhanced fluctuations, non-monotonic cumulants) as the early-warning signal of the endpoint.

### 1.3 Bubble nucleation = metastable-well escape
- Coleman, "The Fate of the False Vacuum. 1. Semiclassical Theory," *Phys. Rev. D* 15 (1977) 2929; Callan & Coleman, part 2, *PRD* 16 (1977) 1762; Linde, finite-T extension, *Nucl. Phys. B* 216 (1983) 421.
- Thin-wall limit: decay rate Γ ~ A exp(−B), with B = 27π²S₁⁴/(2ε³) for wall tension S₁ and vacuum energy splitting ε — **diverging barrier action as ε → 0**: the nucleation rate vanishes as the two wells become degenerate, i.e., the lifetime of the metastable phase diverges approaching the symmetric point. This is precisely the Kramers escape problem in field theory (Langer, *Ann. Phys.* 54 (1969) 258, explicitly framed as "statistical theory of the decay of metastable states").
- **Spinodal limit**: when supercooling drives the false minimum toward the inflection point (barrier → 0), nucleation gives way to **spinodal decomposition** — the direct analog of mechanical snap-through. Guth & Weinberg, *Nucl. Phys. B* 212 (1983) 321 showed old inflation's first-order transition **could not percolate** — a failed regime transition that killed the model.
- Gravitational-wave signatures of first-order transitions (LISA forecasts: Caprini et al., *JCAP* 04 (2016) 001) make bubble nucleation **observationally testable** — prediction-before-collapse in reverse: detecting the fossil record of an ancient snap-through.

## 2. Inflation as Critical Slowing Down of a Scalar Field

- The inflaton obeys φ̈ + 3Hφ̇ + V′(φ) = 0 — a damped particle on a potential. Slow-roll parameters ε = −Ḣ/H², η = V″/(3H²) measure exactly how "slowly rolling" the trajectory is; the slow-roll attractor (Liddle, Parsons, Barrow, *Phys. Rev. D* 50 (1994) 7222, formalized the flow formalism) is a **dynamical attractor**: trajectories with different initial φ̇ converge exponentially — direct analog of overdamped approach to a fold.
- **Inflation ends when ε = 1** — the slow-roll attractor loses stability; the field accelerates, falls into the minimum, and oscillates (reheating). For monomial potentials V ~ φ^p, the end of inflation occurs at a calculable field value — geometrically, the system departs the flat "slow manifold." This is not a fold bifurcation in the strict sense (no coalescing fixed points in the usual framing), but the exit condition ε→1 is a **threshold crossing of a control parameter**, and for hilltop/hybrid inflation (Linde, *Phys. Rev. D* 49 (1994) 748; hybrid inflation ends via a **waterfall field** whose effective mass² goes negative at a critical inflaton value — literally a **spontaneous symmetry breaking triggered as the control parameter crosses zero**, i.e., a genuine bifurcation ending inflation).
- **Critical-slowing-down literalism**: in the Hubble-flow language, the "relaxation time" of φ̇ toward the attractor is ~1/(3H); as ε → 1 the deviation from the attractor grows — the system "heats up" dynamically before the regime change. A rigorous claim that slow-roll *is* critical slowing down requires care: it is better stated as "slow-roll is motion near a slow manifold whose stability is lost at ε=1" (contested framing — flagged as analogy, not established usage).

## 3. Horizons as Information/Calibration Limits

- **Particle horizon**: maximum comoving distance light has traveled since the Bang; defines what is *observable in principle*. **Event horizon**: in accelerating (Λ-dominated) universes, a permanent causal boundary — de Sitter horizon r = c/H ~ 16 Gly, with associated Gibbons–Hawking temperature (Gibbons & Hawking, *Phys. Rev. D* 15 (1977) 2738).
- **Horizon problem**: CMB regions separated by >~2° were never in causal contact in standard Big Bang, yet are at the same temperature to 10⁻⁵ — homogeneity without a causal mechanism. Inflation's solution: expand a causally connected patch beyond the Hubble radius (Guth, *Phys. Rev. D* 23 (1981) 347).
- **Rigorous measurement analogy**: horizons are the cosmological instance of **identifiability limits**. Only quantities inside the particle horizon are calibratable; parameters of the "multiverse" or pre-inflationary initial conditions are *structurally unidentifiable* — not merely imprecise. This is the same logical structure as: a bistable instrument near snap-through cannot calibrate beyond its measurement horizon because recovery time diverges (information return time → ∞ as control → c_snap). In both cases the *quantity of extractable information per unit time collapses at the boundary*. (Analogy rigorous at the level of Fisher information / identifiability theory; flagged as interpretive.)

## 4. Structure Formation: Thresholds, Barrier Crossing, and Caustics

### 4.1 Jeans instability as a threshold phenomenon
- Dispersion relation ω² = c_s²k² − 4πGρ₀; instability for k < k_J = √(4πGρ₀)/c_s (Jeans, 1902). Below the Jeans length: acoustic oscillation; above: **exponential runaway**. This is a clean linear-stability threshold with a control parameter (wavenumber/density) and a critical eigenvalue crossing zero — a **static (zero-frequency) bifurcation** in the infinite-dimensional fluid system. Growth rate near threshold: ω ~ i√(4πGρ₀ − c_s²k²) → the growth time diverges as k → k_J from below: τ_growth ∝ (k_J² − k²)^(−1/2) — **a square-root divergence law at the threshold, structurally identical to the fold law k_eff ~ √(1 − c/c_snap)**.

### 4.2 Press–Schechter: collapse as first-passage / barrier-crossing statistics
- Press & Schechter, *ApJ* 187 (1974) 425: the mass function of collapsed objects = probability that the smoothed density field exceeds the critical overdensity δ_c ≈ 1.686 (spherical collapse threshold). Formalized rigorously as **excursion-set theory** (Bond, Cole, Efstathiou, Kaiser, *ApJ* 379 (1991) 440): trajectories δ(S) vs. smoothing scale S perform a random walk; halo abundance = **first-passage distribution across the barrier δ_c** — mathematically the same first-passage problem as Kramers escape and as hitting-times in regime-shift statistics. Extended to "moving barriers" for ellipsoidal collapse (Sheth & Tormen, *MNRAS* 308 (1999) 119).

### 4.3 Zel'dovich approximation and the Arnold caustic classification — the centerpiece
- Zel'dovich (*A&A* 5 (1970) 84): particles move ballistically along initial displacement, x = q + D(t)·ψ(q). Shell-crossing occurs where the Jacobian det(∂x/∂q) = 0 — the map folds; matter piles up on **caustics** ("pancakes").
- **Arnold, Shandarin, Zel'dovich, "The large-scale structure of the Universe. I. General properties. One-dimensional and two-dimensional models," *Geophysical & Astrophysical Fluid Dynamics* 20 (1982) 111–130** — verified citation (also Arnold's catastrophe-theory program papers 1972–1986). Result: caustics of the Zel'dovich map are Lagrangian singularities of gradient maps; by catastrophe theory, the **generic (structurally stable) caustics in 3D are exactly the fold (A₂), cusp (A₃), swallowtail (A₄), and elliptic/hyperbolic umbilics (D₄±)**. The cosmic web's skeleton is thus *literally classified by catastrophe normal forms*: pancakes are fold surfaces, filaments are cusp lines, nodes are swallowtail/umbilic points.
- Modern revival: Hidding, Shandarin, van de Weygaert, "The Zel'dovich approximation: key to understanding cosmic web complexity," *MNRAS* 437 (2014) 3442, computed the full catastrophe census in cosmological simulations; Feldbrugge & van de Weygaert developed caustic skeleton classification.
- **Implication**: catastrophe theory in cosmology is not metaphor — the A/D-series normal forms are *theorems* about which singularities generic gradient flows produce. Structure formation = the universe computing Thom's classification.

## 5. Dark Energy / Cosmological Constant: The Balance Problem

- **Fine-tuning problem**: QFT vacuum energy estimates exceed the observed ρ_Λ by 60–120 orders of magnitude (Weinberg, "The cosmological constant problem," *Rev. Mod. Phys.* 61 (1989) 1).
- **Coincidence problem**: ρ_matter ∝ a⁻³ vs ρ_Λ = const, yet they are equal within a factor ~2 *today* (Zlatev, Wang, Steinhardt, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 82 (1999) 896).
- **Anthropic bound**: Weinberg (*Phys. Rev. Lett.* 59 (1987) 2607) predicted Λ could not be much larger than observed or galaxy formation is suppressed — a rare successful *a priori* prediction in this area.
- **Contested/current**: DESI 2024–25 BAO results show a mild (~2–3σ) preference for evolving dark energy (w ≠ −1, phantom-crossing); joint Bayesian analyses still support Λ. Hubble tension (~5σ between early- and late-universe H₀) persists (Riess et al. 2022). Status: unresolved; flag both as active.
- **Structural reading**: the coincidence problem asks why we observe the universe *exactly at the fold-crossing epoch* where two scaling regimes exchange dominance — i.e., we live at the regime transition of the cosmic expansion. This is either selection bias (anthropic) or a hint that the transition epoch has special observer-related structure.

## 6. Entropy and Arrow of Time (brief)

- **Past hypothesis**: the second law requires the universe to have started in an extraordinarily low-entropy state (Penrose estimated the phase-space improbability ~1 part in 10^(10^123); Penrose, in *General Relativity: An Einstein Centenary Survey*, 1979). Why entropy was low at the Bang is unexplained by dynamics — it is an *initial-condition postulate*, not a derived result. Contested whether gravity's tendency to clump (increasing gravitational entropy as structure forms) resolves or deepens the puzzle. Boltzmann-brain arguments (Albrecht & Sorbo 2004) show naive de Sitter fluctuation cosmologies are self-undermining — a falsifiability-adjacent constraint on cosmological models.

## 7. Catastrophe and Bifurcation Theory in Cosmology Proper — Verified Uses

1. **Zel'dovich–Arnold caustics** (§4.3) — the flagship, theorem-grade result.
2. **Gravitational lensing**: magnification singularities of lens maps are folds and cusps (Blandford & Narayan 1986; Schneider, Ehlers & Falco, *Gravitational Lenses*, 1992) — observable as fold/cusp image configurations; cusp caustic relations used as tests of substructure.
3. **First-order phase transition thermodynamics**: the finite-T effective potential of gauge–Higgs theory is a Landau free energy with a φ³ term — mathematically a cusp-family unfolding; the EW critical endpoint (Csikor et al. 1999) is an Ising-class critical point.
4. **Hybrid inflation's waterfall** (§2) — a pitchfork-type bifurcation as the mechanism that ends inflation.
5. **Jeans instability** (§4.1) — zero-frequency bifurcation with square-root critical growth law.
6. Cosmological dynamics more broadly uses dynamical-systems phase-space analysis (fixed points of Friedmann equations; e.g., Copeland, Liddle, Wands 1998 on exponential potentials as attractor solutions) — bifurcation methods without always using the name.

## 8. Possible Intersections with the Ecosystem

*Each item: falsifiable claim + concrete test + refutation condition. The creative axis: if Arnold's theorem classifies structure formation by catastrophe normal forms, then the cusp/spinodal toolkit is a STRUCTURE DETECTOR, not only a failure detector.*

### I-1. Fold law at the Jeans threshold
**Claim**: Near the Jeans wavenumber, the gravitational growth timescale follows τ ∝ (k_J² − k²)^(−1/2) — a square-root critical law with exponent 1/2, matching the ecosystem's fold law k_eff ~ √(1 − c/c_snap).
**Test**: Linear perturbation analysis in N-body or hydrodynamic simulations initialized near threshold; fit the divergence exponent.
**Refutation**: Best-fit exponent differs significantly from 1/2 in the asymptotic limit (deviations expected only from nonlinearity/expansion).

### I-2. Caustic census as a cusp-detector benchmark
**Claim**: The ecosystem's cusp-normal-form fitting (spinodal h* = 2/√27) can identify fold and cusp caustics in Zel'dovich-approximation density fields *before* shell-crossing completes, using the deformation-tensor eigenvalue field λ_min(q,t) as the order parameter.
**Test**: Generate 2D/3D Zel'dovich fields with known power spectra; run the cusp/spinodal fitter on λ_min approaching zero; measure lead time vs. naive density-threshold detection.
**Refutation**: The normal-form fit provides no earlier or more specific localization of caustics than a density threshold.

### I-3. Early-warning signals forecast the EW/QCD critical endpoint
**Claim**: Critical slowing down and variance inflation diagnostics applied to heavy-ion fluctuation observables (net-proton cumulants) outperform current changepoint methods at locating a QCD critical endpoint, *if it exists*, because the endpoint is a genuine critical point where these diagnostics are theorem-backed.
**Test**: Apply EWS toolkit to simulated event-by-event cumulant data from hydrodynamic models with a tunable critical point (e.g., BEST equation of state).
**Refutation**: EWS metrics show no monotonic sharpening as the scan trajectory approaches the known critical point.

### I-4. Crossover ≠ no signal: "flickering-before-bifurcation" at the cusp shoulder
**Claim**: The SM electroweak and QCD crossovers — trajectories *past the cusp point but near the fold line* — should show enhanced, non-diverging susceptibility peaks whose shape is predicted by the cusp normal form's distance from the cusp point; the toolkit's "flickering" diagnostic should register a bounded (non-critical) signature distinct from true criticality.
**Test**: Fit cusp-family susceptibility shapes to published lattice QCD crossover data (Borsanyi et al. 2020); check whether the inferred cusp distance matches independent estimates.
**Refutation**: Susceptibility shapes inconsistent with any cusp-family unfolding; toolkit cannot distinguish crossover from true criticality on synthetic data.

### I-5. Bubble nucleation rate as a fold-law escape problem
**Claim**: The thin-wall nucleation exponent B = 27π²S₁⁴/(2ε³) → ∞ as ε → 0 is the field-theoretic instance of "recovery time diverges at the spinodal": the metastable phase's lifetime is a Kramers time diverging at the degeneracy point, with a *measurable critical exponent* (B ~ ε⁻³).
**Test**: Numerical Langevin simulations of 1D φ⁴+φ³ metastable decay across ε; fit the divergence exponent of lifetime vs. ε⁻³ prediction.
**Refutation**: Measured exponent differs from 3 beyond Langer-prefactor corrections.

### I-6. Horizon = identifiability boundary (formal, not observational)
**Claim**: A cosmological parameter is identifiable only if its Fisher information given observations inside the particle horizon is nonzero; the event horizon in Λ-dominated expansion makes late-time information a *decaying* resource — formal analog of diverging recovery time shrinking the effective calibration window.
**Test**: Compute Fisher matrices for dark-energy parameters (w₀, w_a) vs. redshift coverage; show information gain saturates as the event horizon truncates accessible volume.
**Refutation**: Fisher information keeps growing with survey volume at fixed horizon — would falsify the claimed structural role.

### I-7. End of inflation as a stability-loss detector
**Claim**: EWS diagnostics (rising variance, rising lag-1 autocorrelation of the adiabatic curvature perturbation at horizon exit) applied to stochastic- inflation simulations detect the approach to ε = 1 before it occurs in the mean trajectory.
**Test**: Stochastic inflation (δN or Langevin) simulations of monomial and hilltop potentials; compute CSD metrics on the coarse-grained field; measure prediction horizon.
**Refutation**: No leading rise in variance/autocorrelation relative to a null model with constant drift.

### I-8. Press–Schechter barrier = first-passage calibration of the EWS toolkit
**Claim**: Excursion-set first-passage statistics across δ_c provide an analytically known ground truth for validating the ecosystem's barrier-crossing/hitting-time estimators on Gaussian random fields.
**Test**: Generate Gaussian δ(S) walks with known power spectra; compare toolkit-estimated first-passage distributions against analytic Press–Schechter/Sheth–Tormen curves.
**Refutation**: Estimator bias exceeds stated error bars against the analytic benchmark.

### I-9. Cosmic web morphology as normal-form fingerprint
**Claim**: The relative abundance of fold : cusp : swallowtail : umbilic caustics in the Zel'dovich web is a statistic sensitive to the primordial power spectrum (Hidding et al. 2014 shows it is computable); departures from Gaussianity shift the census in a predicted direction.
**Test**: Compute caustic census for Gaussian vs. non-Gaussian (local f_NL) initial conditions; check discriminative power vs. bispectrum.
**Refutation**: Census is statistically indistinguishable between Gaussian and f_NL ~ 100 fields at fixed power spectrum.

### I-10. The coincidence epoch as a fold crossing (speculative, flagged)
**Claim**: The matter–dark-energy equality epoch is a control-parameter crossing where the expansion's effective dynamics changes sign of acceleration; *if* structure-formation observables encode a signature of this crossing (suppressed growth rate fσ₈ near z ~ 0.5–1), then the toolkit's regime-transition detectors should register the crossing in growth-rate data.
**Test**: Apply changepoint/CSD diagnostics to fσ₈(z) compilations; compare detected transition redshift to the ΛCDM equality prediction z ≈ 0.3–0.4.
**Refutation**: Detected changepoint inconsistent with equality epoch, or no better than a null broken-power-law fit.
